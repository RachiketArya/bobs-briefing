🏛️ Regulation · EU DSA

ChatGPT is now a "Very Large Online Search Engine" under EU law — and OpenAI answers for it

The EU has classified ChatGPT under the Digital Services Act's toughest tier, meaning OpenAI must now formally mitigate risks tied to minors, user mental health, and illegal content spread through the product — with regulator audits and reporting obligations attached.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is the clearest precedent yet for "AI product = regulated platform." Any Aspire feature that surfaces AI-generated content or recommendations to end users should assume EU-style systemic-risk obligations are coming to other jurisdictions. Vendor due diligence on OpenAI/Anthropic contracts should now ask directly how DSA compliance costs get passed through.
Source: The Verge →
⚖️ Legal · IP Risk

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic over "tens of thousands" of songs

The two publishers are seeking up to $150,000 per infringed work plus additional statutory damages, in the Northern District of California — the latest and largest music-rights case against a frontier AI lab.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Model-provider IP litigation exposure is now a vendor-risk line item, not a hypothetical. If Aspire's stack has any Anthropic dependency (directly or via a sub-processor), flag it in the next vendor risk review and confirm indemnification language covers training-data litigation, not just output liability.
Source: The Verge →
💰 Funding · Chips

Nvidia puts $3.5B into MediaTek — a hedge against Big Tech building its own AI chips

The investment signals Nvidia's strategy for staying central to AI infrastructure even as hyperscalers push in-house silicon; MediaTek gets capital and a stronger seat in the AI accelerator supply chain.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Concentration risk in the AI compute supply chain is worth a line in any infra vendor review — cost and availability of inference capacity from cloud partners could shift as this consolidation plays out.

🤝 Industry · Governance

OpenAI cuts off Cursor's access to its models after the SpaceX acquisition

OpenAI is winding down the contract that let Cursor route requests to its models, following Cursor's acquisition by SpaceX — a reminder that model access is a revocable commercial relationship, not a guaranteed utility.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A direct case study for concentration risk: any internal tool built on a single model provider's API can lose access overnight following an unrelated corporate event upstream. Worth confirming Aspire's AI-tooling stack has a documented fallback provider.
Source: OpenAI →
📱 Product · Platform Trust

Instagram starts limiting reach for undisclosed AI-generated profiles

Meta is renaming its "AI creator" label to "AI-generated profile" and cutting distribution for accounts that don't disclose they're AI, responding to growing user frustration with AI-influencer slop.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Disclosure-by-default is becoming the platform norm, not just a regulatory ask. Any customer-facing AI persona, chatbot, or synthetic content Aspire ships should already carry clear AI disclosure to stay ahead of this shift rather than react to it.
Source: The Verge →

Video-search startup Clipto hits $250M valuation on $15M ARR — and it's already profitable

Three years old, Clipto lets users search terabytes of video content with AI and raised a fresh $15M round after reaching profitability — a rare combination in the current funding climate.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Profitable-before-raising is becoming the bar investors expect from AI vertical tools; useful benchmark if Aspire evaluates AI-tooling vendors or a build-vs-buy call for internal search/discovery features.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Add a "provider concentration" line to the AI vendor risk register. The Cursor/OpenAI cutoff shows model access can be revoked over an unrelated corporate event — confirm every internal AI tool has a documented fallback provider.

2. Check indemnification language on any Anthropic-touching contract. The Sony/Warner suit puts training-data litigation risk on the table; standard output-liability clauses may not cover it.

3. Audit AI disclosure on customer-facing chat/synthetic content. Platform norms (Instagram) are moving faster than regulation here — get ahead of it rather than reacting to a takedown or a complaint.
