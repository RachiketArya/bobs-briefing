Shopify lets shoppers' AI agents browse and buy directly from its catalog

Shopify announced an expansion letting more third-party AI agents query and purchase from merchant listings on a shopper's behalf, pushing "agentic commerce" from pilot to default distribution channel.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If agents start transacting against merchant catalogs at scale, payment and merchant-risk platforms need agent-identity and spend-authorization controls, not just card-present/not-present logic. 🔐 Security · Hardware supply chain

Ledger confirms a spy chip implant was behind a wave of crypto wallet thefts

Ledger has confirmed that compromised hardware wallets contained a covert implant chip responsible for stolen funds — a hardware supply-chain attack, not a software or seed-phrase failure.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A reminder that hardware custody devices are still a vendor-risk surface: provenance checks on any hardware wallet or HSM procured for custody operations shouldn't stop at firmware signing — physical tamper-evidence and supply chain of custody matter too.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Define an agent-identity policy before agentic commerce volume arrives. Shopify's move means "AI agent acting on a customer's behalf" is now a transaction type merchant-risk and payments teams will see in logs — decide now how it's flagged and authorized rather than retrofitting after an incident.

2. Extend vendor due diligence to physical/hardware provenance, not just firmware signing. The Ledger implant case is a reminder that custody hardware audits need a supply-chain-of-custody check, especially for any device sourced through resellers.

3. Put a name and sign-off step on AI-assisted security triage. Sophos' 96%/52% numbers are a strong case for AI-assisted detection — but make the human sign-off point explicit in the incident-response runbook before adopting similar tooling.
