🧠 Product · Research Tools

Google turns NotebookLM into Gemini Notebook and adds secure code execution inside the notebook

Google is renaming NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook, but the deeper move is infrastructural. Each notebook is being upgraded with a secure cloud computer that can write and execute code against the source material inside the notebook. That pushes it from smart summarizer toward a bounded research environment, which is much more relevant for enterprise use.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
For a regulated fintech, this is the right direction of travel: not just “AI that knows things,” but AI that can analyze sensitive material inside a controlled execution boundary. That boundary, provenance model, and permission layer matter as much as the model itself.

🧪 Research · Open Models

Thinking Machines releases Inkling, a 975B-parameter open-weight multimodal model built around customization

Mira Murati’s Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling, its first in-house model, with a 975 billion parameter mixture-of-experts design that activates roughly 41 billion parameters per task. The company is explicitly arguing against one-size-fits-all AI, positioning Inkling as a model that enterprises can adapt, fine-tune, and own operationally rather than merely rent.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is a reminder that enterprise AI strategy may bifurcate: frontier closed models for high-leverage tasks, and customizable open-weight systems for repeatable internal workflows where data control, cost, and explainability matter more than absolute peak benchmark scores.

🌏 Breaking · Global Competition

China’s Moonshot unveils a massive open model while Xi pushes a more globally accessible AI order

Two related signals landed almost at once: Reuters reports that Moonshot unveiled what it describes as the world’s largest open AI model, while Xi used a Shanghai forum to frame China as a backer of a more open global AI order. The combined message is geopolitical as much as technical: open model leadership is becoming a strategic instrument, not just a product decision.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This matters for vendor strategy. If model quality continues to converge globally, selection criteria for financial institutions will tilt harder toward controllability, legal clarity, deployment economics, and jurisdictional comfort rather than pure frontier prestige.

🏛️ Regulation · Fintech

The UK’s AI Champions want to speed up financial-services adoption with a ten-point plan

The UK government’s financial-services AI Champions published an adoption plan with ten recommendations spanning regulatory clarity, operational resilience, AI-powered advice, skills, and even agentic payments. The subtext is important: regulators and policymakers increasingly want faster AI deployment, but with explicit operating guardrails rather than a free-for-all.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is close to the operating model question for Aspire. Safe acceleration will likely beat cautious stagnation, but only if AI initiatives are tied to clear controls around resilience, human review, incident handling, and product-boundary definitions.

🛡️ Safety · Governance

Anthropic explores a technical “off switch” for dual-use knowledge instead of one fixed model for everyone

Anthropic and AE Studio published research on GRAM, a training approach that routes sensitive dual-use knowledge into removable model modules. In principle, that means one training run could produce multiple safe deployment configurations, instead of forcing firms to choose between a fully capable model and a heavily filtered one. It is still early research, but the governance idea is striking: capability segmentation at the model-weight level.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If this line of research matures, it could become highly relevant for regulated AI deployment. The dream scenario for finance is not generic blanket refusal behavior, but configurable access to sensitive capabilities based on environment, user trust, and task class.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Separate AI surfaces by execution rights. Distinguish plain-language assistants from tools that can run code, touch internal systems, or call third-party services.

2. Decide where open-weight models are strategically useful. Some internal workflows may benefit more from controllability and private tuning than from renting the strongest general model.

3. Build a policy for configurable capability access. Even if weight-level segmentation is early, product teams can already design environment-based permissions, approval ladders, and kill switches.

4. Watch global vendor exposure. As model supply diversifies, legal jurisdiction, resilience, and data-governance posture should become first-class procurement criteria.

5. Assume supervisors will get more model-native. Logging, explainability, and review paths should be written for both internal governance and future machine-assisted regulator scrutiny.
