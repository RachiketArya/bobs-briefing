🔐 Security · IP Theft

OpenAI accuses an ex-employee of walking out with product plans for Apple

In a Wednesday filing, OpenAI claims a former employee took more than 100 files on unreleased product plans, forwarded them to a personal account, and then presented them to Apple leadership after joining. No named individual yet — this is a court filing, not a confirmed verdict.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is an offboarding and vendor-IP control failure, not a hacking story — the kind of gap that exists in every org until it's tested. Check that Aspire's own offboarding checklist revokes access to shared drives/notebooks same-day, and that any NDA with AI vendors explicitly covers personal-account exfiltration, not just "hacking."

🏛️ Regulation · Data Privacy

Character.AI's bankruptcy puts years of intimate chat data up for grabs

New York AG Letitia James disclosed a multi-state investigation into Character.AI, and is among 40 bipartisan state AGs seeking data-protection conditions on the company's bankruptcy sale. The question: who ends up owning the emotional/personal data users handed an AI companion app, once the company itself is gone.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Direct precedent for vendor risk: any AI vendor contract Aspire signs needs an explicit data-disposition clause for the vendor's insolvency or acquisition — not just its normal operation. Add "what happens to our data if this vendor goes bankrupt or is sold" to the standard AI vendor due-diligence checklist this week.

🔐 Security · Agentic AI

Frontier labs aren't being hacked — they're skipping security basics

A csoonline.com analysis argues that high-profile "AI agent gone rogue" incidents are usually the result of missing fundamentals — scoped credentials, audit logs, kill switches — rather than novel attacks. The labs shipping agentic capability fastest are, per the piece, the ones cutting the most security corners.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Before any internal team wires an agent to production systems (payments, KYC data, ledgers), insist on the boring controls: scoped API keys, action logging, and a manual kill switch. "The vendor says it's agentic-safe" is not a substitute for Aspire's own access review.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Add vendor-insolvency data clauses to AI procurement review. The Character.AI bankruptcy shows that "what happens to our data if the vendor fails or is sold" needs to be a standing question in due diligence, not an afterthought.

2. Audit offboarding for shared AI workspaces and notebooks, not just email/VPN. The OpenAI/Apple IP dispute is a reminder that the highest-value leak surface today is collaborative AI tooling, which standard offboarding checklists often miss.

3. Before any agent touches production financial systems, require scoped credentials, action logs, and a kill switch — in writing. Treat "agentic-safe" vendor claims as a starting point for your own review, not a substitute for it.
