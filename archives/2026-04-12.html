Anthropic Launches Project Glasswing — Claude Mythos Preview Finds Thousands of Zero-Day Vulnerabilities

This is a big one. Anthropic unveiled Project Glasswing on April 7, deploying its unreleased frontier model Claude Mythos Preview specifically for defensive cybersecurity. Launch partners include AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Cisco — essentially every major infrastructure player. The model autonomously identifies software vulnerabilities faster than human security researchers, compressing discovery timelines from months to minutes. Anthropic reports thousands of high-severity zero-day bugs found across every major OS and browser. On benchmarks, Mythos Preview hits 93.9% on SWE-bench Verified and 94.6% on GPQA Diamond. The dual-use tension is obvious — the same model that finds vulnerabilities could theoretically exploit them — but Anthropic is positioning this as "offense informs defense" with strict access controls and partner vetting.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Two angles for Monday. First, vendor security: if Aspire uses any software from Apple, Google, Microsoft, or AWS (spoiler: you do), the bugs Glasswing is finding are in your stack. The good news is the responsible-disclosure pipeline means patches will flow. The strategic question is whether to proactively ask key vendors about their Glasswing participation in your next security review. Second, AI governance: Glasswing is a textbook case of a dual-use AI system. Your AI Use Policy should have a position on whether Aspire would ever deploy autonomous vulnerability-discovery tools — and under what guardrails. MAS will care about this.

🚀 Product · Autonomous

Netherlands Becomes First European Country to Approve Tesla FSD Supervised

On April 10, the Dutch vehicle authority RDW granted Tesla formal type approval for FSD Supervised under UN Regulation 171 — the EU standard governing automated driving systems. This makes the Netherlands the first European country to authorize the system, opening the door to a broader EU rollout this summer. Separately, Tesla also received regulatory approval for unsupervised FSD pilot programs in London and Berlin. The distinction matters: "supervised" requires driver attention; the UK/Germany pilots are testing unsupervised operation. The regulatory patchwork is messy but moving fast — and the AI driving the system is fundamentally a vision-language model making real-time safety decisions. This is AI governance with actual body-count stakes.

⚖️ Policy · Economics

OpenAI Publishes "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age" — Robot Taxes, Wealth Funds, and a 32-Hour Workweek

Sam Altman released a 13-page policy blueprint this week that reads like a New Deal for the AI era. The headline proposals: taxes on automated labor, shifting the tax base from payroll toward capital gains and corporate income (since AI could hollow out wage-based revenue funding Social Security and Medicaid), a public wealth fund modeled on Alaska's Permanent Fund to give every citizen a "stake in AI-driven economic growth," and a pilot 32-hour workweek funded by AI productivity gains. The timing — ahead of OpenAI's expected IPO — is strategically impeccable. Whether you read it as genuine policy thought-leadership or pre-IPO reputation management, the document is now the most specific economic framework any AI lab has proposed. Governments will respond.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
The "automated labor tax" proposal directly affects fintech. If AI handles KYC checks, fraud monitoring, or credit decisions that previously required human analysts, the fiscal argument is that the productivity surplus should be taxed differently. This won't be law soon — but it signals the policy direction. For Aspire's AI strategy deck, it's worth a slide on "regulatory trajectory risk" that flags this framework. Your board will see it in the FT and ask about it. Better to have the answer ready.

🏭 Infrastructure · Chips

Intel Joins Musk's Terafab — The AI Chip Mega-Fab Gets Its Manufacturing Partner

Announced April 7 and still reverberating: Intel formally joined Elon Musk's Terafab project, a joint venture between SpaceX, xAI, and Tesla to build a next-generation AI chip fabrication facility. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan hosted Musk at Intel facilities before the announcement, and Intel's contribution is clear: manufacturing expertise for ultra-high-performance chips at scale. The joint-venture structure shares capital risk and chip output across stakeholders. Musk stressed that the majority of chip output would serve SpaceX for satellite-based AI data centers and a "space-oriented industrial economy." This is vertical integration taken to its logical extreme — own the model, own the cloud, own the fab, own the orbit.
