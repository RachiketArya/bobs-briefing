OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and 100+ others form a coalition to defend against rogue AI

The largest AI labs and tech companies announced a joint initiative to shore up cybersecurity as AI-driven attacks escalate. It's the clearest signal yet that the vendors themselves see current safeguards as insufficient, and are choosing self-organized defense over waiting for regulation.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
When vendors admit their own models are attack vectors, vendor-risk assessments need a new question: not just "does this model get abused by third parties" but "has this vendor's model been directly implicated in an attack." Ask AI vendors for their incident history, not just their SOC2.

🏢 Industry · Talent

Barret Zoph's whirlwind year: Thinking Machines co-founder → brief OpenAI stint → now at Google

Zoph co-founded Thinking Machines Lab with Mira Murati and served as its CTO, then moved to OpenAI, and has now landed at Google — three moves inside roughly a year. It's one of the starkest data points yet on how unstable the top of the AI talent market has become.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If we depend on any vendor's "star" research team for a roadmap commitment, this is a reminder that key-person risk at frontier labs is real and fast-moving — build vendor continuity into contracts, not just pricing.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Add an "AI incident history" line to vendor due diligence. With 100+ companies now organizing around rogue-AI defense and TechCrunch running a documented incident log, ask every AI vendor directly whether their models have been implicated in an attack — don't assume "no news" means "no incident."

2. Draw the line between "AI advises" and "AI executes" in any agentic tool we pilot. Google's AI Mode moving into bookings is the same pattern we'll face internally — before any agent gets write access to a system that moves money or commits the company, require an explicit human approval gate, documented, not implied.

3. Flag key-person concentration risk in AI vendor contracts. The Zoph story is a reminder that frontier-lab talent churn is fast; if a vendor pitch leans on "our top researcher," ask what continuity commitment exists if that person leaves within the contract term.
