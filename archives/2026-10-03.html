🔒 Security · Platform Controls

Apple tightens macOS Full Disk Access specifically to blunt AI agent risk

Apple confirmed it is adding new controls around macOS's Full Disk Access permission, explicitly citing the risk that increasingly capable AI agents pose when given broad access to a user's files, Mail, Messages, and browsing history.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A platform vendor now treating "AI agent with broad filesystem access" as its own threat category is a signal worth folding into endpoint policy — any agent tooling given Mac access (including Adam-style assistants) should be reviewed against the new permission tier once it ships, not grandfathered in under old disk-access grants.
Source → TechCrunch

Chatham Financial cuts trade validation from 30 minutes to under 4 using Codex and GPT-5.6

Chatham Financial, a capital-markets risk advisory, used Codex and GPT-5.6 to rebuild internal workflows and redesign trade validation — cutting a 30-minute manual process down to under 4 minutes, per OpenAI's customer writeup.

🧮 Research · Capital Flows

New NBER paper: 2-6% of World Bank foreign aid got siphoned into crypto wallets

A new NBER working paper estimates that between 2% and 6% of World Bank foreign aid disbursements ended up diverted into crypto wallets, using on-chain tracing to follow the money after it left official channels.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Not AI, but directly relevant to governance: on-chain tracing of diverted development funds is a template for the kind of transaction-monitoring rigor regulators will increasingly expect from any platform touching cross-border payment rails.
Source → NBER (via HN)
🏗️ Industry · Infrastructure Politics

Amazon publishes 3,000-word blog telling communities not to block data centers

AWS CEO Matt Garman posted a lengthy defense of AI data-center buildout, warning that local opposition to new sites risks "irreparable harm to the US economy and national security" — an unusually direct public pressure campaign from a hyperscaler.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A core cloud vendor publicly fighting local permitting pushback is an early signal of real compute/power supply constraints ahead — worth factoring into any multi-year infrastructure or AI-vendor capacity planning rather than assuming current provisioning holds.
Source → The Verge

Only 2% of consumers are buying the "super intelligence" rebrand

Days after the White House got Zuckerberg, Bezos, Musk, and Anthropic's Dario Amodei to sign a "morally binding" AI safety pledge and started calling frontier AI "super intelligence," new survey data shows just 2% of consumers are actually buying the rebrand.
