🔐 Security · Data Practices

Unsealed filings show Microsoft privately called AI scraping "theft" while doing it anyway

Newly unredacted court filings reveal a Microsoft exec called AI data scraping "the largest theft of labor in human history" internally — while Microsoft and OpenAI both built training datasets from paywalled New York Times content and warned it would gut publishers.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Any vendor using LLM-derived features on customer or market data should be asked directly what training data provenance controls exist. This filing is discovery evidence that even top-tier vendors knew their sourcing was legally exposed — a due-diligence question worth adding to vendor risk questionnaires now, before it becomes a headline liability inherited by a partner.

🧠 Research · Safety

AI labs are quietly abandoning "move fast" for a safety-first posture

After a summer of rogue-agent incidents and researcher warnings, The Verge reports leading US AI labs are now publicly walking back "move fast and break things" in favor of deliberate pacing — a marked culture shift from a year ago.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If frontier labs are decelerating release cadence for safety review, expect longer model-update cycles and more pre-release gating from vendors. Build that lag into any roadmap that depends on next-gen model capability — don't plan integrations assuming quarterly leaps continue at the old pace.

🏛️ Regulation · Public Opinion

Pew: AI is now feared globally as a job destroyer, across 37 countries

A Pew survey of 42,151 people across 37 countries (fielded Feb–May, before recent apocalyptic AI headlines) finds broad global anxiety about AI's effect on jobs and income inequality — a sentiment baseline that's likely worsened since.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Public sentiment this negative typically precedes regulatory action and workforce-disclosure requirements. Any AI-driven automation in customer-facing or headcount-sensitive processes should have a change-communication plan ready, not just a technical rollout plan.

⚡ Product · Agents

Claude Code relaunches "Projects" to run fleets of cloud agents under one roof

Anthropic's revamped Projects feature lets teams run multiple Claude Code agents concurrently with shared memory, goals, and a common file/artifact library — organized into "threads," echoing similar multi-agent management tools from Grok and others.

🏦 Aspire Relevance

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Add a data-provenance question to vendor due diligence. The Microsoft/OpenAI filings show even top-tier vendors knowingly used legally exposed training data — ask any AI vendor directly how their model's training data was sourced and licensed.

2. Treat model-release cadence as a planning variable, not a constant. Safety-driven slowdowns at frontier labs mean roadmap dependencies on "next model version" should carry longer buffers than they did a year ago.

3. If piloting multi-agent coding tools, get an audit-trail requirement in writing first. Shared-memory agent fleets (Claude Code Projects and similar) need logging/oversight defined before rollout, not retrofitted after.
