💰 Funding · OpenAI

OpenAI reportedly raises another $11B at a $1.25T valuation

A new tranche is reportedly closing in the coming weeks, pushing OpenAI's valuation to $1.25 trillion even as the company's compute and infrastructure commitments keep climbing. The raise underscores how much private capital is still willing to underwrite frontier-model burn rates.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Vendor concentration risk: any fintech with OpenAI in its critical path is leaning on a company whose balance sheet is now inseparable from one investor cycle. Revisit model-provider redundancy and exit clauses before the next funding headline becomes a service-continuity headline.
Source → TechCrunch
🔓 Security · Exposed Tooling

Hundreds of Artex AI hacking tools found exposed to the open internet

Security researchers found hundreds of servers running the AI-assisted penetration-testing tool Artex left publicly reachable, handing anyone drive-by access to an offensive hacking toolkit. It's the same pattern seen with past pentest/C2 frameworks — powerful tooling, sloppy deployment hygiene.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If any internal red-team or vendor pentest workflow touches AI-assisted offensive tooling, confirm it never sits on an internet-routable box without auth. This is exactly the kind of exposure a regulator or auditor would flag in a vendor security review.
Source → TechCrunch
🔬 Research · Self-Improvement

Anthropic report: AI is now building AI roughly twice as fast as a year ago

A new METR-published report found Anthropic is using AI extensively in its own model development, with improvement velocity running about 2x faster than a year ago and no clear sign of slowing. Researchers in AI safety circles are calling the finding alarming because it compresses the window for external oversight.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Model-capability jumps are arriving faster than vendor-risk review cycles can track them. Governance teams should move model re-assessment from an annual cadence to a quarterly one for any AI vendor in the critical path.
Source → The Verge
🏛️ Regulation · Export Controls

The US reportedly let Nvidia sell AI chips to an empty office in China

The New York Times reports that New Matrix, a firm cleared by the Commerce Department to receive Nvidia's H200 chips, operates out of what appears to be an empty office — raising questions about how rigorously both the US government and Nvidia vet buyers on the approved-export list.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A live case study in know-your-counterparty failure at the compliance layer. Worth citing in any internal training on third-party due diligence — "on the approved list" is not the same as "verified."
Source → The Verge
🏢 Industry · Enterprise AI

Goldman Sachs is reportedly pulling Claude Code from its bankers and traders

Per a Hacker News-surfaced report, Goldman Sachs is walking back Claude Code access for front-office staff — one of the more visible signs yet that a large regulated financial institution is recalibrating how much autonomy to grant coding agents inside trading and banking workflows.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If a bank of Goldman's scale is tightening agentic-coding access in regulated functions, it's a useful data point for Aspire's own AI tool-access policy review — enthusiasm for agents in finance is cooling exactly where the stakes are highest.
Source → The State of AI
