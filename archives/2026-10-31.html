🔒 Security · Supply chain

A backdoored Claude Code extension sat on Cursor's marketplace

Researchers found a compromised version of the official Claude Code for VS Code extension distributed through the Cursor Marketplace — a reminder that dev-tool marketplaces for AI coding assistants are now an active attack surface, not a trusted default.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Any engineer at Aspire installing AI coding extensions from a secondary marketplace (vs. the official VS Code/JetBrains store) is a live supply-chain risk. Worth a one-line policy update: AI dev-tool extensions only from first-party marketplaces, verified publisher IDs checked.

Anthropic's own IPO filing admits the AI boom could be "catastrophic"

Anthropic's S-1 risk factors section states plainly that its technology could, in a worst case, "pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" — an unusually candid admission from a company simultaneously asking public markets to fund it at a trillion-dollar scale.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A frontier vendor's own SEC filing naming existential risk as a disclosed factor strengthens the case for contractual indemnity and model-risk clauses in any Aspire-Anthropic commercial agreement — this is now citable, public-record language for vendor risk committees.

🧠 Research · Infrastructure

Google's first AI data-center satellite is online — and writing poetry

Google booted up the TPUs on its first orbital data-center satellite under Project Suncatcher, running a Gemma 4 model that generated its first response — a haiku — and transmitted it back to Earth, validating space-based compute as more than a thought experiment.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Early-stage, but it signals where hyperscaler compute capacity planning is heading as terrestrial power and water constraints bite (see the Maine data-center item below). Worth tracking for multi-year infra and data-residency assumptions, not an action item today.

🏢 Industry · Trust & ethics

Theranos whistleblower: the AI boom is "a great opportunity to go deceive people"

Tyler Shultz, who helped expose Theranos, is drawing explicit parallels between that fraud and today's AI funding environment, where pre-revenue or unprofitable companies are raising capital at trillion-dollar valuations on the strength of a narrative.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A useful external gut-check for any Aspire due diligence on AI vendors or investment targets: valuation and hype are not evidence of durable technology. Diligence teams should keep demanding audited usage and revenue data, not just growth narratives, before committing capital or contracts.
