China Rolls Out Sweeping AI Ethics Framework — 10 Agencies, Three-Tier Governance

On April 3, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, together with nine other government agencies, jointly issued the Administrative Measures for the Ethical Review and Services of Artificial Intelligence Science and Technology (Trial). The framework introduces a three-tier governance structure: internal ethics committees within organizations, external service centers, and government-led expert review for high-risk projects. AI technologies with capabilities for public opinion mobilization, autonomous decision-making, or deep human-machine integration are classified as "high-risk" and require mandatory third-party expert review. Crucially, the system now links algorithm filing with ethical evaluation in a dual-track access model. New "algorithm auditing" mechanisms targeting platform sectors like ride-hailing mandate human override functions to prevent "algorithmic exploitation" of workers.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is a significant signal for any fintech operating in or adjacent to the Chinese market. The dual-track "algorithm filing + ethical evaluation" model could become a template other APAC regulators follow. The mandatory human override for platform algorithms is directly relevant to AI-driven lending, credit scoring, and fraud detection systems. Worth tracking for Aspire's AI governance framework — this is the direction regulatory winds are blowing.

2026 National AI Policy Framework: Federal Preemption Over State AI Laws

The new 2026 National AI Policy Framework establishes federal preemption over the patchwork of state AI regulations that had been proliferating across the US. The framework aims to create a unified regulatory calculus for AI value creation while defending free speech and preventing AI censorship. Meanwhile, Morgan Lewis reports that AI enforcement is actually accelerating despite the federal framework — agencies are using existing statutes for enforcement while states continue expanding AI laws under antitrust, consumer protection, and false claims provisions. The gap between policy intent and enforcement reality is widening.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
The federal preemption angle is significant for any fintech with US exposure. A unified federal framework should simplify compliance vs. navigating 50 different state AI rules — but the enforcement acceleration means the rules already on the books are being applied more aggressively. For Aspire's AI governance work, monitor both the new framework AND existing regulatory enforcement actions.
