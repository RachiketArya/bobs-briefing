🛡️ Security · Research

Hundreds of "Artex" AI hacking-tool servers left wide open on the internet

Security researchers found hundreds of servers running the Artex penetration-testing tool exposed with no authentication, giving anyone who found them free access to an AI-assisted hacking toolkit. It's the same pattern that's dogged Shodan-discoverable dev tools for years, now with an AI offensive-security layer bolted on.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Any AI pentest or red-team tool Aspire evaluates needs a default-closed network posture confirmed in writing, not assumed — "AI-powered" vendors are shipping the same basic exposure mistakes as everyone else, just with sharper tools in the box when someone stumbles onto them.

🏛️ Regulation · Politics

Voters said no to data centers almost everywhere they got to vote on it

Tuesday's US midterms carried 41 local data-center ballot measures — in all but one, the majority voted for tighter restrictions on new builds. Paired with the Democratic sweep of both chambers, it's the clearest signal yet that AI infrastructure buildout has become a live local-politics fight, not just a boardroom capex line.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Cloud/AI vendor pricing assumptions in any 2027 roadmap should get a sensitivity check now — if siting and power approvals slow across dozens of jurisdictions simultaneously, compute cost and availability risk moves from theoretical to a line item worth flagging upward.

Ramp's card data shows AI spend stalling — right as Anthropic nears IPO

Ramp's transaction data shows AI usage still climbing but corporate spend on AI tools flattening, as price cuts and tighter procurement controls kick in among the biggest buyers. The timing matters: the stall lands just as Anthropic reportedly approaches IPO territory, where growth-rate scrutiny gets a lot sharper.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If AI vendor spend is flattening industry-wide, that's leverage Aspire hasn't had yet in renewal conversations — this is a good month to push back on list-price increases from any AI vendor citing "hypergrowth" as justification.

🔐 Security · Partnership

OpenAI brings in outside hackers to attack its own systems

OpenAI is partnering with Hacktron to probe for vulnerabilities, stress-test third-party service connections, and harden defenses using frontier AI as part of the offense. It's a direct, practical follow-on to the GPT-6 Cyber launch earlier this week.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Worth asking any AI vendor in due diligence whether they run an equivalent continuous red-team program against their own integrations — "we have a bug bounty" and "we actively hire hackers to break our production systems" are different levels of assurance.

🔬 Research · Benchmark

OpenAI builds a benchmark for AI agents doing actual lab science

BioBasicsBench is a new 100-task benchmark measuring how well AI agents handle the unglamorous mechanics of wet-lab science — reading raw experimental data correctly, not just generating plausible-sounding hypotheses. It's a tell that frontier labs are getting serious about agents operating in physical-world-adjacent, error-costly domains.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
The benchmark's whole premise — agents fail quietly on raw data handling even when their narrative output looks fine — is the same failure mode worth testing for in any AI-assisted reconciliation or document-processing pipeline Aspire runs on real transaction data.
