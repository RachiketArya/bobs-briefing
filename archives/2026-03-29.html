Judge Blocks Pentagon Ban on Anthropic — Calls It "First Amendment Retaliation"

Federal Judge Rita Lin granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction against the Trump administration's order labeling the company a "supply chain risk." In a blistering ruling, Lin wrote: "Nothing in the governing statute supports the Orwellian notion that an American company may be branded a potential adversary and saboteur of the U.S." The dispute arose after Anthropic publicly criticized the Pentagon's AI contracting terms. This is a landmark case for AI companies' right to speak critically about government policy without retaliatory consequences.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This ruling is significant for any company in the AI supply chain — it establishes that government agencies can't punish vendors for public criticism. For Aspire's AI governance framework, this case is worth tracking as it defines the boundary between vendor advocacy and regulatory risk.

Capacity Wars

Anthropic Throttles Claude During Peak Hours — OpenAI Pounces

Anthropic announced it's now throttling Claude usage during peak hours (5–11am PT weekdays), burning through 5-hour session limits faster during those windows. The reasoning: explosive demand is outstripping compute capacity. OpenAI immediately seized the opening, with marketing targeting frustrated Claude users. Business Insider noted that tools like OpenClaw are enabling users to "tap into the full potential of AI models like never before" — driving unprecedented usage. Meanwhile, Anthropic's paid subs have more than doubled this year. Growth problems are the best kind of problems.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Capacity constraints at frontier AI providers are real and getting worse. If Aspire is building on Claude or similar APIs, plan for rate-limiting scenarios and consider multi-provider fallback strategies. Peak-hour throttling will only intensify as agentic use cases grow.
