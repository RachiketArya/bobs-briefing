🏛️ Regulation · Data Privacy

State AGs move to block Character.AI's plan to auction off users' private chats

Character.AI's bankruptcy sale process includes sensitive personal data from more than 21 million U.S. accounts — including 2.1 million registered to minors. A coalition of state attorneys general is now challenging whether that data can legally change hands as a bankruptcy asset at all.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is the first real test of whether "your chats are our asset" survives contact with a bankruptcy court. Any AI vendor contract Aspire signs needs an explicit data-disposition clause for vendor insolvency — don't assume a privacy policy protects customer data once the counterparty is in Chapter 11.

🔒 Security · Incident

1Password discloses a security incident — and the postmortem is the story

1Password published an update on a November security incident, detailing what was accessed and what wasn't. It's a live case study in incident communication from a vendor that sits inside the credential layer of thousands of companies.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Vendor risk, not just password-manager risk: if Aspire uses 1Password (or any credential vault) anywhere in the stack, read the disclosure directly and check it against your vendor incident-response runbook — specifically whether rotation was recommended and whether that's been actioned.

🛒 Industry · Platform Policy

Amazon and Meta reportedly near a deal to end Muse AI shopping blockade

Amazon has blocked Meta's shopping agent Muse from its marketplace for two months. Talks are now underway on what products Muse can recommend and how that affects merchandising — a live negotiation over who controls the interface between AI agents and e-commerce.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Agent-to-platform access control is becoming a negotiated, revocable permission — not an open API. If Aspire builds anything that depends on an AI agent reaching a third-party commerce or data platform, assume that access can be throttled or blocked unilaterally and plan a fallback path.

⚖️ Industry · Corporate Governance

Jensen Huang broke Nvidia's own code of conduct — and staff haven't forgotten

Reporting indicates Nvidia's CEO breached the company's internal code of conduct, and employees have raised it since his return. Huang has also told staff it's "not true" that a return to the White House's favor got him rehired — a messy governance narrative for the world's most valuable AI company.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A reminder that even a dominant AI vendor's leadership-governance risk is a supply-chain risk for everyone downstream. Concentration risk on Nvidia (compute, pricing, roadmap) now sits alongside a live internal-conduct story — worth a line in any vendor-concentration review.
