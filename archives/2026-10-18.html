🚨 Breaking · Model Vendor Switch

Replit drops Anthropic, makes OpenAI its default model provider

Replit CEO Amjad Masad confirmed over the weekend that OpenAI will replace Anthropic as the platform's default model, though Claude stays available to customers who select it. No pricing or performance rationale was disclosed publicly.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A reminder that "default model" is a business decision, not a technical constant — vendors switch underlying providers without much warning. If Aspire builds on any platform's default AI tier rather than pinning a specific model, build in a review step before assuming behavior, cost, or output quality stays stable release to release.

🏛️ Regulation · Content Rights

Meta removes language that may have let it scrape news sites for AI training

After public backlash — including coverage flagged in this briefing yesterday — Meta quietly updated its commercial terms again, removing wording that suggested it could use content from sites, including news publishers, to train its AI without separate permission.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
The reversal came from public pressure, not regulatory action — a useful data point on how fast platform data-rights terms can move in either direction. Worth tracking Meta's Business terms on a recurring basis rather than a one-time legal read, since this is the second change inside two weeks.
Source: The Verge →
🏦 Product · Regulated Fintech

How Itaú is building generative AI for its entire customer base

Brazil's largest private bank has rolled out ia.i, a conversational interface for customers, alongside IARA, an internal governance platform for employees — applying an "AI First" strategy across one of Brazil's most tightly regulated sectors, not just a pilot team.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
This is the closest direct comparable in this briefing to date: a regulated financial institution shipping customer-facing AI with a paired internal governance layer, not AI and governance as separate workstreams. Worth a deliberate read for Aspire's AI roadmap — specifically how IARA's governance model handles audit trail, explainability, and employee oversight at scale.

💳 Product · Credit & Lending

Benefit Street Partners answers loan-valuation questions faster with Codex

Analyst Anant Kumar built internal tools using Codex to compare loan valuations and analyze credit agreements, speeding up code review and follow-up questions on deals — a direct-lending credit fund using an AI coding tool for analytical, not just engineering, work.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A concrete pattern for Aspire's credit/risk teams: coding-assistant tools applied to build lightweight internal analysis tooling (valuation comparisons, agreement parsing) rather than general-purpose chat. Low-risk entry point if scoped to internal tooling with no client data exposure, and worth a pilot proposal to risk/credit leadership.

🌐 Industry · Agent Access

Websites are closing their doors to AI agents

As autonomous agents from OpenAI, Meta, Google and others promise to browse and transact on users' behalf, a growing number of sites are actively blocking them — raising the question of what "agentic browsing" is actually worth if much of the web refuses to cooperate.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If Aspire is evaluating or building any agent that browses third-party sites on a customer's behalf (price checks, account aggregation, bill pay), budget for a non-trivial failure rate against sites that actively fence agents out — and don't commit to a UX promise ("the agent will handle that for you") the open web can't reliably support yet.

Aspire Governance Corner
📋 Suggested moves for this week
1. Pin model versions, don't rely on platform defaults. Replit's Anthropic-to-OpenAI switch shows default-tier AI behavior can change under a vendor without notice — any Aspire workflow depending on a platform's "default model" should pin or at least monitor for silent provider swaps.

2. Put Meta's Business terms on a recurring review cadence. Two changes to scraping language inside two weeks means a one-time legal read isn't enough — schedule a quarterly check if Aspire runs any Meta Business/Page presence.

3. Study Itaú's ia.i + IARA pairing as a governance reference. It's the most directly comparable regulated-fintech AI rollout surfaced so far — worth a short internal writeup on what their customer-facing and internal-governance split implies for Aspire's own AI roadmap.
