💰 Funding

OpenAI raises another $11B, pushing its valuation to $1.25 trillion

OpenAI has reportedly raised an additional $11 billion in a round that hasn't closed yet, taking its valuation to $1.25 trillion — up from $852 billion just this past March. The pace of re-pricing is now measured in months, not years, and it's happening right as Anthropic reportedly edges toward its own IPO.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Any multi-year AI vendor contract priced off current OpenAI/Anthropic economics should get revisited every two quarters, not annually — valuations this volatile are a proxy for pricing and roadmap volatility downstream, including for Aspire's own integrations.

🤝 Acquisition · Infrastructure

Crusoe sells its troubled Wyoming data-center project to Google

Crusoe has sold a Wyoming AI data-center development that ran into serious trouble to Google, highlighting just how hard it has become to build hyperscale AI infrastructure on schedule — even for well-funded specialists. Expect more of these bailout-style transfers as compute buildout outpaces execution capacity.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
If any Aspire roadmap assumes a specific vendor's compute capacity timeline, build in a fallback — troubled builds changing hands mid-construction is now a normal failure mode, not an edge case, for AI infrastructure delivery.

🔬 Research

Anthropic's own researchers are alarmed by how fast AI is now building AI

A new METR report shows Anthropic's internal AI-development velocity improving at roughly twice the rate it did a year ago, with little sign of slowing — and it's Anthropic's own safety researchers flagging the acceleration as worth watching closely, not outside critics.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Model capability jumps are compounding faster than most vendor-risk review cycles run — worth moving any AI model/vendor re-assessment cadence from annual to semi-annual, especially for anything touching customer data or decisioning.

🏛️ Regulation · Security

The US let Nvidia sell AI chips to what looks like an empty office in China

A firm called New Matrix is on the US government's approved list to receive Nvidia's export-controlled H200 chips — but reporting suggests its listed office is empty, raising questions about whether Commerce or Nvidia did real due diligence before clearing the sale. It's a concrete crack in the export-control vetting process, not a hypothetical one.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
A useful prompt for any compliance review: if a government export-control process can miss an empty-office counterparty, Aspire's own third-party/KYC vetting on AI vendors with cross-border operations deserves the same skepticism — don't assume a regulator's clearance is a substitute for independent diligence.
Source: The Verge →
🕵️ Security · Espionage

China reportedly recruited an AI executive to spy on Nvidia's Jensen Huang

China's Ministry of State Security allegedly enlisted Megaspeed executive Alice Huang to gather information on American AI chip strategy and potentially influence Nvidia's push against export controls, per the New York Times. It's a reminder that the AI chip war has a human-intelligence layer running alongside the trade-policy one.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Any Aspire staff with visibility into AI vendor roadmaps or chip-supply strategy should be reminded that competitive intelligence approaches from "AI industry contacts" are a live threat vector, not paranoia — basic info-sharing hygiene matters here.

📊 Industry

Microsoft Research quietly winds down its AI Frontiers group

Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group — one of its flagship frontier-AI research efforts — is reportedly ending, a sign that even the biggest, best-funded labs are consolidating research bets rather than running parallel frontier tracks indefinitely.

🏦 Aspire Relevance
Worth checking whether any Aspire AI tooling depends on research output or APIs tied specifically to a lab group like this one — research-team closures are an early warning sign for feature deprecation or support quality drops six to twelve months out.
